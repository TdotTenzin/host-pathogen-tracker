PathoMap

How are microbial genomes, biological interactions, evolution, and ecological environments connected? PathoMap traces those links — start from a pathogen and follow it out through its effectors, the host proteins they manipulate, and the evolutionary relationships between them. Genome, pangenome, and ecological-niche layers are on the roadmap.

Built by a student focused on cellular microbiology and host–pathogen dynamics

Interactome Analysis

Host–Pathogen Interaction Network

Force-directed view of how a pathogen's effector repertoire targets host proteins. Select a pathogen to see effectors (colored) connecting to the host proteins (blue) they manipulate. Hover to highlight a node's connections and drag nodes to rearrange the layout.

Select a pathogen above to visualize its interaction network.

Each edge represents an effector–host protein interaction. The 150 interaction records collapse to 135 distinct edges here, because the graph is simple and a few effector–protein pairs are recorded more than once. Effectors are colored by the selected pathogen; hovering a node dims unrelated connections. The network is derived from the curated effector dataset and works fully offline.

Cellular Context

Phagosome Maturation Timeline

      Research Inspiration

      Inspired by Foundational Work

      Mauricio Terebiznik Lab · University of Toronto Scarborough

      This site is inspired by research on host–pathogen interactions and intracellular trafficking in labs such as Mauricio Terebiznik's at the University of Toronto Scarborough. The Terebiznik laboratory investigates how intracellular pathogens — particularly Salmonella, Legionella pneumophila, and Helicobacter pylori — remodel host cell membranes, co-opt vesicular trafficking machinery, and survive within phagocytic compartments. Their work bridges quantitative fluorescence imaging with cell biology and microbial pathogenesis.

      PathoMap

      A browsable curated dataset: 54 pathogens, their effectors, the host proteins those effectors manipulate, and the phagosome stages they exploit. Every table is a SQL query; the projections, classifier and phylogeny are computed in Python. Built for advanced undergraduate biology students.
