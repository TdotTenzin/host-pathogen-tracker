Interactome Analysis

Host–Pathogen Interaction Network

Force-directed view of how a pathogen's effector repertoire targets host proteins. Select a pathogen to see effectors (colored) connecting to the host proteins (blue) they manipulate. Hover to highlight a node's connections and drag nodes to rearrange the layout.

Select a pathogen above to visualize its interaction network.

Each edge represents an effector–host protein interaction. Effectors are colored by the selected pathogen; hovering a node dims unrelated connections. Network is derived from the curated effector dataset and works fully offline.

Cellular Context

Phagosome Maturation Timeline

A phagosome matures through sequential fusion events: Rab5/EEA1 early endosomes, then Rab7/LAMP1 late endosomes, finally a degradative phagolysosome (pH drops from ~6.5 to ~4.5). Click a stage to see its molecular markers, the cellular changes, and which pathogens manipulate exactly that step.

      Effector Protein Phylogeny

      Research Inspiration

      Inspired by Foundational Work

      Mauricio Terebiznik Lab · University of Toronto Scarborough

      This site is inspired by research on host–pathogen interactions and intracellular trafficking in labs such as Mauricio Terebiznik's at the University of Toronto Scarborough. The Terebiznik laboratory investigates how intracellular pathogens — particularly Salmonella, Legionella pneumophila, and Helicobacter pylori — remodel host cell membranes, co-opt vesicular trafficking machinery, and survive within phagocytic compartments. Their work bridges quantitative fluorescence imaging with cell biology and microbial pathogenesis.
