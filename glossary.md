# Glossary of Key Terms

## Tier 1 — Foundational Microbiology & Cell Biology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Bacterium** | A single-celled prokaryotic microorganism lacking a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles. Bacteria are classified by shape (cocci, bacilli, spirilla), Gram-stain reactivity (Gram-positive vs. Gram-negative), and metabolic strategy. |
| **Gram-positive** | Bacteria with a thick peptidoglycan cell wall that retains crystal violet stain, appearing purple. Examples: *Listeria monocytogenes*, *Staphylococcus aureus*. |
| **Gram-negative** | Bacteria with a thin peptidoglycan layer and an outer membrane containing lipopolysaccharide (LPS); do not retain crystal violet and appear pink/red after Gram staining. Examples: *Salmonella enterica*, *Shigella flexneri*, *Legionella pneumophila*. |
| **Acid-fast** | Bacteria whose waxy, mycolic acid-rich cell wall resists decolorization by acid-alcohol after carbolfuchsin staining. The primary human pathogen with this property is *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. |
| **Eukaryotic cell** | A cell with a membrane-bound nucleus, internal organelles (ER, Golgi, lysosomes, mitochondria), and an endomembrane system. Host cells in infection — macrophages, epithelial cells — are eukaryotic. |
| **Prokaryotic cell** | A cell lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Bacteria are prokaryotes. |
| **Pathogen** | A microorganism capable of causing disease in a host. Pathogenicity depends on the microbe's virulence factors and the host's immune status. |
| **Virulence** | The degree of pathogenicity — the capacity of a microorganism to cause damage to the host. Virulence factors include toxins, adhesins, secretion systems, and effector proteins. |
| **Macrophage** | A professional phagocytic immune cell that ingests and destroys pathogens, dead cells, and debris. Macrophages are central to innate immunity and are a primary niche for many intracellular pathogens. |
| **Neutrophil** | The most abundant white blood cell; a short-lived phagocyte that uses ROS, proteases, and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) to kill pathogens. |
| **Epithelial cell** | A cell lining the surfaces of organs, skin, and mucous membranes. Many pathogens (e.g., *Salmonella*, *Shigella*) invade epithelial cells to establish infection. |
| **Actin** | A globular protein that polymerizes into filaments (F-actin), forming the major component of the cytoskeleton. Drives membrane extension during phagocytosis and is hijacked by pathogens (*Listeria* ActA, *Shigella* IcsA) for intracellular motility. |
| **Cytosol** | The aqueous fluid phase of the cytoplasm surrounding organelles. Several pathogens (*Listeria*, *Shigella*) escape the phagosome to replicate in the cytosol. |
| **Microtubules** | Hollow cytoskeletal filaments (α/β-tubulin polymers) that serve as tracks for vesicle transport. Dynein and kinesin motors move cargo along microtubules toward or away from the microtubule-organizing center (MTOC). |
| **E-cadherin** | A host cell-cell adhesion molecule on epithelial cells. Used by *Listeria monocytogenes* invasin InlA as a receptor for entry. |
| **Met receptor** | Hepatocyte growth factor receptor on host cells. Targeted by *Listeria monocytogenes* invasin InlB to trigger PI3K and Rac1 signaling for zipper-mediated entry. |
| **PI3K (Phosphoinositide 3-kinase)** | A host lipid kinase that phosphorylates phosphoinositides to generate signaling lipids. Activated during *Listeria* entry and manipulated by various pathogens to control trafficking. |
| **Rac1** | A host Rho-family small GTPase that regulates actin polymerization. Activated downstream of PI3K during *Listeria* zipper-mediated entry. |
| **Complement receptor 3 (CR3)** | A host phagocytic receptor (integrin αMβ2, CD11b/CD18) that binds complement-opsonized particles. Used by *M. tuberculosis* for entry without triggering a strong oxidative burst. |
| **Mannose receptor** | A host C-type lectin receptor on macrophages that recognizes mannosylated ligands. Used by *M. tuberculosis* as an alternative entry route that bypasses classical FcR-mediated antimicrobial signaling. |

## Tier 2 — Endomembrane System & Vesicular Trafficking

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Endocytosis** | The process by which cells internalize extracellular material by invaginating the plasma membrane to form an intracellular vesicle. Includes phagocytosis, pinocytosis, and receptor-mediated endocytosis. |
| **Exocytosis** | The fusion of intracellular vesicles with the plasma membrane to release cargo to the extracellular space. |
| **Phagocytosis** | Receptor-mediated engulfment of large particles (>0.5 µm) by specialized cells (macrophages, neutrophils, dendritic cells). Driven by actin polymerization and membrane remodeling. |
| **Phagosome** | The membrane-bound vacuole formed around a particle after phagocytosis. The phagosome matures through sequential fusion and fission events with endosomes and lysosomes. |
| **Phagolysosome** | The fully mature, degradative phagosome formed after fusion with lysosomes. Characterized by an acidic pH (~4.5–5), active hydrolytic enzymes (cathepsins, lipases, nucleases), and reactive oxygen species. |
| **Endosome** | A membrane-bound compartment of the endocytic pathway. Early endosomes receive incoming cargo and sort it; late endosomes prepare cargo for degradation. |
| **Early endosome** | The first endocytic compartment after the plasma membrane. Marked by Rab5, EEA1, and phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PI3P). pH ~6.0–6.5. |
| **Late endosome** | A pre-lysosomal compartment. Marked by Rab7, LAMP1, and intraluminal vesicles. pH ~5.0–5.5. |
| **Lysosome** | The terminal degradative organelle containing acid hydrolases. pH ~4.5. Fuses with late phagosomes to form phagolysosomes. |
| **Lysosome-Associated Membrane Protein 1 (LAMP1)** | A heavily glycosylated transmembrane protein enriched in late endosomes and lysosomes. Used as a marker for phagolysosome maturation. |
| **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)** | An organelle involved in protein synthesis, folding, and lipid biosynthesis. Some pathogens (e.g., *Legionella*) recruit ER-derived vesicles to their vacuole. |
| **Golgi Apparatus** | The organelle that modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids for transport to their destinations. The trans-Golgi network (TGN) is a hub for vesicle sorting. |
| **Vacuolar H⁺-ATPase (V-ATPase)** | A multi-subunit proton pump that acidifies intracellular compartments (phagosomes, lysosomes, endosomes). Its exclusion from a pathogen-containing vacuole prevents acidification. |
| **Mannose-6-Phosphate Receptor (M6PR)** | A receptor that transports hydrolytic enzymes from the Golgi to lysosomes. Pathogens like *Salmonella* exclude M6PR from their vacuole to prevent hydrolase delivery. |
| **Intracellular Trafficking** | The tightly regulated network of vesicle budding, transport, and fusion events that moves membrane cargo between organelles. Governed by Rab GTPases, SNAREs, coat proteins, and phosphoinositides. Central to pathogen survival strategies. |
| **Phosphoinositides** | Phosphorylated derivatives of phosphatidylinositol that mark organelle identity and regulate membrane trafficking. Key species include PI3P (early endosomes), PI(4,5)P₂ (plasma membrane), and PI(4)P (Golgi). Manipulated by pathogens to control phagosome maturation. |
| **Coiling phagocytosis** | A variant of phagocytosis where the host membrane wraps around the target in multiple layers, forming a coil-like structure. Characteristic of *Legionella pneumophila* uptake in some host cells. |

## Tier 3 — Molecular Machinery of Trafficking

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Rab GTPase** | A family of small (~21 kDa) GTP-binding proteins that serve as molecular switches controlling vesicle identity, budding, transport, tethering, and fusion. Each organelle has a characteristic Rab signature. |
| **Rab5** | The master regulator of early endosomes. Recruits effectors (EEA1, Rabex-5, Vps34) that promote PI3P production and early endosome fusion. |
| **Rab7** | The master regulator of late endosomes and lysosomes. Recruits RILP to connect to dynein-dynactin motors for centripetal movement along microtubules and fusion with lysosomes. |
| **Rab conversion** | The coordinated exchange of Rab5 for Rab7 on maturing endosomes/phagosomes. Mediated by the Mon1-Ccz1 complex (a Rab7 GEF) and SAND-1, which displaces Rab5. |
| **Rab1** | A Rab GTPase that regulates ER-to-Golgi vesicular transport. Hijacked by *Legionella pneumophila* via the effector DrrA/SidM. |
| **Rab9** | A Rab GTPase involved in retrograde transport of M6PR from late endosomes to the TGN. Target of *Salmonella* effector SifA. |
| **Rab35** | A Rab GTPase regulating endocytic recycling and actin dynamics. Targeted by *Legionella* effector AnkX for phosphocholination. |
| **SNARE proteins** | Soluble N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive-factor attachment protein receptors. Mediate membrane fusion by forming a four-helix bundle (trans-SNARE complex) between vesicle and target membranes. |
| **EEA1** | Early Endosome Antigen 1 — a PI3P-binding tethering factor that mediates early endosome fusion. Used as a marker for early phagosomes. |
| **RILP** | Rab7-Interacting Lysosomal Protein — an effector of Rab7 that recruits dynein-dynactin motors, driving phagosomes toward lysosomes for fusion. |
| **ORP1L** | Oxysterol-Binding Protein-Related Protein 1 — a Rab7 effector that mediates cholesterol sensing and endoplasmic reticulum contacts at late endosomes. Manipulated by *Salmonella* to decouple LAMP1 acquisition from full maturation. |
| **PI3P (Phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate)** | A phosphoinositide lipid enriched on early endosomes and phagosomes. Recruits EEA1, Hrs, and other PI3P-binding proteins essential for maturation. Depleted by *M. tuberculosis* SapM phosphatase. |
| **PI(4,5)P₂ (Phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate)** | A plasma-membrane-enriched phosphoinositide involved in actin polymerization and signaling. Hydrolyzed during phagocytosis. |
| **Vps34** | A class III PI3-kinase that produces PI3P on early endosomes and phagosomes. Recruited by Rab5. |
| **Coat proteins (COPI, COPII, Clathrin)** | Protein complexes that mold membranes into transport vesicles. COPII drives ER-to-Golgi transport; COPI drives Golgi-to-ER retrieval; clathrin mediates endocytosis and TGN-to-endosome traffic. |
| **Arp2/3 complex** | Actin-related protein 2/3 complex — nucleates branched actin filaments. Exploited by *Listeria* (ActA) and *Shigella* (IcsA) for cytoplasmic motility. |
| **Dynein-dynactin** | A microtubule-associated motor complex that drives retrograde (minus-end-directed) transport toward the microtubule-organizing center. RILP-dependent recruitment brings phagosomes to lysosomes. |
| **Kinesin-1** | A plus-end-directed microtubule motor. Recruited by *Salmonella* PipB2 to extend Salmonella-induced filaments (SIFs). |

## Tier 4 — Pathogen Subversion Strategies

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Type III Secretion System (T3SS)** | A needle-like apparatus used by Gram-negative bacteria to inject effector proteins directly into the host cell cytosol. Essential for *Salmonella* (SPI-1, SPI-2), *Shigella*, and others. |
| **Type IV Secretion System (T4SS)** | A secretion system related to bacterial conjugation. The Dot/Icm T4SS of *Legionella pneumophila* translocates ~330 effectors into host cells. |
| **Salmonella Pathogenicity Island 1 (SPI-1)** | A genomic island encoding T3SS-1 and effectors (SopE, SipA, SipC) that mediate bacterial entry into non-phagocytic cells via a trigger/ruffle mechanism. |
| **Salmonella Pathogenicity Island 2 (SPI-2)** | A genomic island encoding T3SS-2 and effectors (SifA, SopD2, SseJ, SseF/G) required for intracellular survival and SCV maintenance. |
| **Salmonella-Containing Vacuole (SCV)** | The membrane-bound compartment where *Salmonella* resides and replicates. Acquires LAMP1 and Rab7 but excludes M6PR and cathepsins — a unique non-degradative LAMP1⁺ niche. |
| **Salmonella-Induced Filaments (SIFs)** | Long tubular membrane extensions emanating from the SCV. Enriched in LAMP1; function in nutrient acquisition and SCV membrane maintenance. Require SifA for formation. |
| **SifA** | A SPI-2 T3SS effector essential for SCV integrity and SIF formation. Binds SKIP, displaces Rab9 to block M6PR trafficking, and recruits LAMP1 to the SCV. Contains a WxxxE motif. |
| **SKIP (PLEKHM2)** | SifA and Kinesin-Interacting Protein — a host adaptor linking SifA to kinesin-1 and Rab9. SifA-SKIP binding sequesters Rab9 to block M6PR recycling. |
| **SopB (SigD)** | A SPI-1 T3SS effector with phosphatidylinositol phosphatase activity. Depletes PI(4,5)P₂ at invasion sites and recruits Rab5/Vps34 to produce PI3P on the SCV. |
| **SopE** | A SPI-1 T3SS effector of *Salmonella* that acts as a GEF for host Rho-family GTPases (Cdc42, Rac1), triggering actin rearrangements and membrane ruffling for bacterial entry. |
| **SipA** | A SPI-1 T3SS effector of *Salmonella* that binds and stabilizes actin filaments, enhancing the efficiency of actin polymerization during the trigger/ruffle entry mechanism. |
| **Listeriolysin O (LLO)** | A cholesterol-dependent cytolysin secreted by *Listeria monocytogenes*. Pore-forming toxin with an acidic pH optimum (~5.5); inactive at neutral pH, providing a built-in safety switch after phagosomal escape. |
| **ActA** | A *Listeria* surface protein that mimics host WASP-family proteins. Nucleates Arp2/3-dependent actin polymerization at the bacterial pole, propelling the bacterium through the cytosol and into neighboring cells. |
| **InlA / InlB** | *Listeria* invasins. InlA binds E-cadherin; InlB binds the Met receptor (hepatocyte growth factor receptor). Trigger zipper-mediated entry via PI3K and Rac1 signaling. |
| **Lipoarabinomannan (LAM)** | A major glycolipid of *M. tuberculosis* that inhibits Ca²⁺ signaling and blocks Vps34 recruitment, preventing PI3P production on the phagosome. |
| **SapM** | A secreted acid phosphatase of *M. tuberculosis* that dephosphorylates PI3P on the phagosomal membrane, preventing Rab5→Rab7 conversion and arresting maturation. |
| **MptpA / MptpB** | *M. tuberculosis* protein tyrosine phosphatases. MptpA blocks V-ATPase trafficking; MptpB hydrolyzes PI(3,5)P₂. Work with SapM to enforce phagosome maturation arrest. |
| **ESX-1 (Type VII Secretion System)** | A secretion system in *M. tuberculosis* that permeabilizes the phagosomal membrane, enabling effectors like SapM to access host cytosolic targets. |
| **Legionella-Containing Vacuole (LCV)** | The replicative niche of *Legionella pneumophila*. An ER-derived compartment that bypasses the endocytic pathway entirely, built by Dot/Icm T4SS effectors. |
| **Dot/Icm** | Defect in organelle trafficking / Intracellular multiplication — the T4SS of *Legionella pneumophila*. Translocates ~330 effector proteins into host cells. |
| **DrrA / SidM** | A *Legionella* Dot/Icm effector with dual GEF and GDI-displacement factor (GDF) activity toward Rab1. Also AMPylates Rab1. Recruits ER-derived COPII vesicles to the LCV. |
| **AnkX** | A *Legionella* effector with FIC domain that phosphocholinates Rab1 and Rab35 using CDP-choline. Prevents GEF activation and GDI-mediated extraction, locking Rabs in an inactive membrane-associated state. |
| **Lem3 (Lpg0696)** | A *Legionella* dephosphocholinase that reverses AnkX-mediated phosphocholination of Rab1, providing temporal control of the modification during infection. |
| **SidD** | A *Legionella* deAMPylase that reverses DrrA/SidM-mediated AMPylation of Rab1. |
| **LidA** | A *Legionella* Dot/Icm effector that binds Rab1 on the LCV, stabilizing its active GTP-bound form and protecting it from host GAPs. |
| **IpaB** | A *Shigella flexneri* T3SS translocator and pore-forming protein. Inserts into the phagosomal membrane to mediate vacuolar lysis and escape. Also activates caspase-1 in macrophages. |
| **IpaC** | A *Shigella* T3SS translocator that works with IpaB to form the translocon pore and induces actin polymerization for bacterial entry. |
| **IcsA (VirG)** | A *Shigella* outer membrane autotransporter that recruits N-WASP and Arp2/3 to the bacterial pole, driving actin-based motility and cell-to-cell spread. |
| **IcsB** | A *Shigella* T3SS effector that masks IcsA from autophagy receptor LC3 and prevents septin cage entrapment. Also functions in escape from double-membrane vacuoles during cell-to-cell spread. |
| **VirA** | A *Shigella* T3SS effector with Rab1 GAP activity. Facilitates escape from LC3-positive vacuoles formed during intercellular spread. |
| **Septin cage** | A host cytoskeletal structure composed of septin filaments that entraps cytosolic *Shigella*, restricting actin-based motility and targeting bacteria for autophagy. |
| **Xenophagy** | A form of selective autophagy that targets intracellular pathogens for degradation. Ubiquitin, adaptors (p62, NDP52), and LC3 are recruited to bacteria. |

## Tier 5 — Experimental Techniques & Analysis

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Fluorescence microscopy** | A technique using fluorophores (GFP, mCherry, antibodies) to visualize specific proteins or structures in fixed or live cells. Used to track phagosome markers and pathogen localization. |
| **Confocal microscopy** | A fluorescence microscopy method using a pinhole to reject out-of-focus light, generating optical sections. Essential for resolving membrane boundaries and 3D reconstruction of phagosome-organelle contacts. |
| **Widefield fluorescence** | A microscopy method where the entire sample is illuminated; faster and higher throughput than confocal but includes out-of-focus blur. Suitable for endpoint colocalization assays. |
| **Colocalization analysis** | A quantitative method to measure the spatial overlap between two fluorescence channels. Indicates whether two proteins (e.g., a pathogen and LAMP1) occupy the same subcellular compartment. |
| **Pearson's correlation coefficient** | A colocalization metric ranging from -1 to +1 that measures the linear correlation between pixel intensities in two channels. Values >0.5 indicate significant colocalization. |
| **Manders' colocalization coefficients** | Coefficients (M1, M2) that quantify the fraction of one channel's signal that overlaps with the other. Less sensitive to intensity variations than Pearson's. |
| **Costes threshold** | An automated thresholding method that removes random overlap from colocalization analysis by comparing to pixel-shuffled images. Reduces noise bias. |
| **Galectin-3 (GAL3) puncta** | A marker for damaged membranes. Galectin-3 binds exposed β-galactoside residues on the luminal face of damaged vacuoles. Used to confirm phagosomal escape by pathogens. |
| **Live-cell imaging** | Time-lapse microscopy of living cells, tracking dynamic processes like Rab conversion, phagosome movement, and pathogen escape in real time. |
| **Fluorescence tagging** | The fusion of a fluorescent protein (GFP, mCherry, mNeonGreen) to a protein of interest (e.g., GFP-Rab5, mCherry-LAMP1) to visualize its localization and dynamics. |
| **Immunofluorescence** | Detection of proteins in fixed cells using primary antibodies and fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies. Allows multiplexed labeling of host and pathogen proteins. |
| **RNA interference (RNAi)** | A method to knock down gene expression using small interfering RNAs (siRNAs). Used to test the functional role of host factors (e.g., SKIP, Rab proteins) in infection. |
| **Z-stack** | A series of optical sections acquired at different focal planes. Reconstructed into a 3D volume to analyze phagosome position, shape, and contact sites. |
