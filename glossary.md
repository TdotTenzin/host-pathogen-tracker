# Glossary of Key Terms

## Tier 1 — Foundational Microbiology & Cell Biology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Bacterium** | A single-celled prokaryotic microorganism lacking a membrane-bound nucleus and organelles. Bacteria are classified by shape (cocci, bacilli, spirilla), Gram-stain reactivity (Gram-positive vs. Gram-negative), and metabolic strategy. |
| **Gram-positive** | Bacteria with a thick peptidoglycan cell wall that retains crystal violet stain, appearing purple. Examples: *Listeria monocytogenes*, *Staphylococcus aureus*. |
| **Gram-negative** | Bacteria with a thin peptidoglycan layer and an outer membrane containing lipopolysaccharide (LPS); do not retain crystal violet and appear pink/red after Gram staining. Examples: *Salmonella enterica*, *Shigella flexneri*, *Legionella pneumophila*. |
| **Acid-fast** | Bacteria whose waxy, mycolic acid-rich cell wall resists decolorization by acid-alcohol after carbolfuchsin staining. The primary human pathogen with this property is *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. |
| **Eukaryotic cell** | A cell with a membrane-bound nucleus, internal organelles (ER, Golgi, lysosomes, mitochondria), and an endomembrane system. Host cells in infection — macrophages, epithelial cells — are eukaryotic. |
| **Prokaryotic cell** | A cell lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Bacteria are prokaryotes. |
| **Pathogen** | A microorganism capable of causing disease in a host. Pathogenicity depends on the microbe's virulence factors and the host's immune status. |
| **Virulence** | The degree of pathogenicity — the capacity of a microorganism to cause damage to the host. Virulence factors include toxins, adhesins, secretion systems, and effector proteins. |
| **Macrophage** | A professional phagocytic immune cell derived from monocytes. Ingests and destroys pathogens, dead cells, and debris via phagolysosomal degradation. Produces cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) to recruit other immune cells. Central to innate immunity and a primary niche for intracellular pathogens — *Salmonella*, *Mycobacterium*, *Leishmania*, and *Legionella* all replicate inside macrophages. Polarisation state (M1 pro-inflammatory vs. M2 anti-inflammatory) affects pathogen survival. |
| **Neutrophil** | The most abundant white blood cell (40–70% of circulating leukocytes). Short-lived (hours to days) but rapidly recruited to infection sites. Kills via ROS (NADPH oxidase), proteases (elastase, cathepsin G), and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) — webs of decondensed chromatin studded with antimicrobial proteins. Targeted by *Staphylococcus aureus* (protein A), *Streptococcus* (M protein), and *Yersinia* (YopH). |
| **Epithelial cell** | A polarised cell lining the surfaces of organs, skin, and mucous membranes. Apical surfaces face the lumen; basolateral surfaces face underlying tissue. Many pathogens (*Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Campylobacter*) invade epithelial cells via specific invasins. Epithelial cells produce antimicrobial peptides (defensins) and cytokines, making them active immune sentinels. |
| **Dendritic cell** | An antigen-presenting cell that bridges innate and adaptive immunity. Captures pathogens in peripheral tissues, processes their proteins, and migrates to lymph nodes to present antigens to T cells via MHC molecules. Some pathogens (*Mycobacterium*, *Leishmania*) survive inside dendritic cells, potentially subverting adaptive immunity. |
| **Monocyte** | A circulating leukocyte that differentiates into macrophages or dendritic cells upon tissue entry. Recruited to infection sites by chemokines (CCL2, CX3CL1). Inflammatory monocytes (Ly6C-high in mice) are rapidly recruited; patrolling monocytes (Ly6C-low) maintain vascular integrity. |
| **Innate immunity** | The first line of defence: non-specific, rapid (minutes to hours), and does not generate immunological memory. Includes physical barriers (skin, mucosa), phagocytic cells (macrophages, neutrophils), complement system, and cytokine signalling. All pathogens in this database must evade innate immunity to establish infection. |
| **Adaptive immunity** | The specific, slower (days) immune response involving T and B lymphocytes. Generates immunological memory (vaccination basis). T cells kill infected cells (CD8+) or coordinate responses (CD4+); B cells produce antibodies. Some pathogens evade adaptive immunity by hiding inside cells (*Mycobacterium*) or antigenic variation (*Trypanosoma*). |
| **Autophagy** | A conserved cellular degradation pathway where cytoplasmic contents are engulfed by double-membrane autophagosomes and delivered to lysosomes. Xenophagy (see Tier 4) is the autophagic targeting of intracellular pathogens. *Shigella* (IcsB), *Listeria* (ActA), and *Salmonella* (SseL) all have mechanisms to evade autophagy. |
| **Actin** | A globular protein that polymerizes into filaments (F-actin), forming the major component of the cytoskeleton. Drives membrane extension during phagocytosis and is hijacked by pathogens (*Listeria* ActA, *Shigella* IcsA) for intracellular motility. |
| **Cytosol** | The aqueous fluid phase of the cytoplasm surrounding organelles. Several pathogens (*Listeria*, *Shigella*) escape the phagosome to replicate in the cytosol. |
| **Microtubules** | Hollow cytoskeletal filaments (α/β-tubulin polymers) that serve as tracks for vesicle transport. Dynein and kinesin motors move cargo along microtubules toward or away from the microtubule-organizing center (MTOC). |
| **E-cadherin** | A host cell-cell adhesion molecule on epithelial cells. Used by *Listeria monocytogenes* invasin InlA as a receptor for entry. |
| **Met receptor** | Hepatocyte growth factor receptor on host cells. Targeted by *Listeria monocytogenes* invasin InlB to trigger PI3K and Rac1 signaling for zipper-mediated entry. |
| **PI3K (Phosphoinositide 3-kinase)** | A host lipid kinase that phosphorylates phosphoinositides to generate signaling lipids. Activated during *Listeria* entry and manipulated by various pathogens to control trafficking. |
| **Rac1** | A host Rho-family small GTPase that regulates actin polymerization. Activated downstream of PI3K during *Listeria* zipper-mediated entry. |
| **Complement receptor 3 (CR3)** | A host phagocytic receptor (integrin αMβ2, CD11b/CD18) that binds complement-opsonized particles. Used by *M. tuberculosis* for entry without triggering a strong oxidative burst. |
| **Mannose receptor** | A host C-type lectin receptor on macrophages that recognizes mannosylated ligands. Used by *M. tuberculosis* as an alternative entry route that bypasses classical FcR-mediated antimicrobial signaling. |

## Tier 2 — Endomembrane System & Vesicular Trafficking

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Endocytosis** | The process by which cells internalize extracellular material by invaginating the plasma membrane to form an intracellular vesicle. Includes phagocytosis, pinocytosis, and receptor-mediated endocytosis. |
| **Exocytosis** | The fusion of intracellular vesicles with the plasma membrane to release cargo to the extracellular space. |
| **Phagocytosis** | Receptor-mediated engulfment of large particles (>0.5 µm) by specialized cells (macrophages, neutrophils, dendritic cells). Driven by actin polymerization and membrane remodeling. |
| **Phagosome** | The membrane-bound vacuole formed around a particle after phagocytosis. The phagosome matures through sequential fusion and fission events with endosomes and lysosomes. |
| **Phagolysosome** | The fully mature, degradative phagosome formed after fusion with lysosomes. Characterized by an acidic pH (~4.5–5), active hydrolytic enzymes (cathepsins, lipases, nucleases), and reactive oxygen species. |
| **Endosome** | A membrane-bound compartment of the endocytic pathway. Early endosomes receive incoming cargo and sort it; late endosomes prepare cargo for degradation. Endosomal maturation involves Rab conversion (Rab5→Rab7), luminal acidification, and progressive loss of recycling receptors. |
| **Early endosome** | The first endocytic compartment after the plasma membrane. Marked by Rab5, EEA1, and phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PI3P). pH ~6.0–6.5. Functions as a sorting station: recycling receptors return to the surface; cargo destined for degradation moves to late endosomes. Characterised by a tubulo-vesicular morphology. |
| **Late endosome** | A pre-lysosomal compartment marked by Rab7, LAMP1, and intraluminal vesicles (ILVs). pH ~5.0–5.5. Multivesicular bodies (MVBs) are a specialised form where ILVs form by inward budding of the limiting membrane. Late endosomes fuse with lysosomes to complete cargo degradation. |
| **Lysosome** | The terminal degradative organelle containing ~60 acid hydrolases (cathepsins, lipases, nucleases, glycosidases). pH ~4.5 maintained by V-ATPase. Lysosomes fuse with late phagosomes to form phagolysosomes. Also serve as signalling hubs (mTORC1 on lysosomal surface senses amino acids). |
| **Phagosome** | The membrane-bound vacuole formed around a particle after phagocytosis. Initially inherits plasma membrane components, then matures through sequential fusion and fission events with endosomes and lysosomes. Maturation markers appear in sequence: Rab5 → Rab7, EEA1 → LAMP1. Pathogens manipulate this pathway at specific steps. |
| **Phagolysosome** | The fully mature, degradative phagosome formed after fusion with lysosomes. Characterized by an acidic pH (~4.5–5), active hydrolytic enzymes (cathepsins B, D, L; lipases; nucleases), and reactive oxygen species (via NADPH oxidase and inducible nitric oxide synthase). The terminal degradative compartment that most pathogens must avoid. |
| **Retrograde transport** | The movement of vesicles from endosomes back toward the Golgi or ER. M6PR recycles from late endosomes to the trans-Golgi network (TGN) to pick up more hydrolases. *Salmonella* SifA blocks this by displacing Rab9, preventing M6PR recycling and hydrolase delivery to the SCV. |
| **Lysosome-Associated Membrane Protein 1 (LAMP1)** | A heavily glycosylated transmembrane protein enriched in late endosomes and lysosomes. Used as a marker for phagolysosome maturation. |
| **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)** | An organelle involved in protein synthesis, folding, and lipid biosynthesis. Some pathogens (e.g., *Legionella*) recruit ER-derived vesicles to their vacuole. |
| **Golgi Apparatus** | The organelle that modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids for transport to their destinations. The trans-Golgi network (TGN) is a hub for vesicle sorting. |
| **Vacuolar H⁺-ATPase (V-ATPase)** | A multi-subunit proton pump that acidifies intracellular compartments (phagosomes, lysosomes, endosomes). Its exclusion from a pathogen-containing vacuole prevents acidification. |
| **Mannose-6-Phosphate Receptor (M6PR)** | A receptor that transports hydrolytic enzymes from the Golgi to lysosomes. Pathogens like *Salmonella* exclude M6PR from their vacuole to prevent hydrolase delivery. |
| **Intracellular Trafficking** | The tightly regulated network of vesicle budding, transport, and fusion events that moves membrane cargo between organelles. Governed by Rab GTPases, SNAREs, coat proteins, and phosphoinositides. Central to pathogen survival strategies. |
| **Phosphoinositides** | Phosphorylated derivatives of phosphatidylinositol that mark organelle identity and regulate membrane trafficking. Key species include PI3P (early endosomes), PI(4,5)P₂ (plasma membrane), and PI(4)P (Golgi). Manipulated by pathogens to control phagosome maturation. |
| **Coiling phagocytosis** | A variant of phagocytosis where the host membrane wraps around the target in multiple layers, forming a coil-like structure. Characteristic of *Legionella pneumophila* uptake in some host cells. |

## Tier 3 — Molecular Machinery of Trafficking

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Rab GTPase** | A family of small (~21 kDa) GTP-binding proteins that serve as molecular switches controlling vesicle identity, budding, transport, tethering, and fusion. Each organelle has a characteristic Rab signature. Active (GTP-bound) Rabs recruit effector proteins on the membrane surface; inactive (GDP-bound) Rabs are extracted by GDI (GDP-dissociation inhibitor) for cytosolic storage. Over 60 Rabs in humans, with specific organelle localisations. |
| **Rab5** | The master regulator of early endosomes. Recruits effectors (EEA1, Rabex-5, Vps34) that promote PI3P production and early endosome fusion. Active Rab5 on the phagosomal membrane signals "early" identity and drives homotypic fusion of early endosomes. Converted to Rab7 during maturation. |
| **Rab7** | The master regulator of late endosomes and lysosomes. Recruits RILP to connect to dynein-dynactin motors for centripetal movement along microtubules and fusion with lysosomes. Also recruits ORP1L for ER contact and cholesterol sensing. The Rab5→Rab7 switch is the critical step in phagosome maturation — many pathogens block it. |
| **Rab conversion** | The coordinated exchange of Rab5 for Rab7 on maturing endosomes/phagosomes. Mediated by the Mon1-Ccz1 complex (a Rab7 GEF) and SAND-1, which displaces Rab5. This switch commits the compartment to late-endosome/lysosome fusion. Occurs ~10–15 minutes after phagocytosis. Blocked by *M. tuberculosis* (SapM depletes PI3P, preventing Rab5 effectors from recruiting Mon1-Ccz1). |
| **Rab1** | A Rab GTPase that regulates ER-to-Golgi vesicular transport. Hijacked by *Legionella pneumophila* via the effector DrrA/SidM, which acts as a GEF to activate Rab1 on the LCV, redirecting COPII vesicle traffic from ER to the pathogen vacuole. |
| **Rab9** | A Rab GTPase involved in retrograde transport of M6PR from late endosomes to the trans-Golgi network (TGN). Target of *Salmonella* effector SifA — SifA-SKIP binding sequesters Rab9, blocking M6PR recycling and preventing hydrolase delivery to the SCV. |
| **Rab35** | A Rab GTPase regulating endocytic recycling and actin dynamics. Targeted by *Legionella* effector AnkX for phosphocholination using CDP-choline, which locks Rab35 in an inactive membrane-associated state. |
| **SNARE proteins** | Soluble N-ethylmaleimide-sensitive-factor attachment protein receptors. Mediate membrane fusion by forming a four-helix bundle (trans-SNARE complex) between vesicle (v-SNAREs) and target membranes (t-SNAREs). NSF ATPase disassembles spent SNARE complexes for recycling. Rab GTPases regulate SNARE complex assembly specificity — different Rabs recruit different SNAREs to ensure correct fusion partners. |
| **EEA1** | Early Endosome Antigen 1 — a PI3P-binding tethering factor that mediates early endosome fusion. Forms homodimers that bridge two membranes via Rab5 interaction. Used as a marker for early phagosomes. Absent from late phagosomes after Rab conversion. |
| **RILP** | Rab7-Interacting Lysosomal Protein — an effector of Rab7 that recruits dynein-dynactin motors via its C-terminal domain, driving phagosomes toward the perinuclear region for lysosomal fusion. Also interacts with the HOPS tethering complex to promote SNARE-mediated fusion. |
| **ORP1L** | Oxysterol-Binding Protein-Related Protein 1 — a Rab7 effector that mediates cholesterol sensing and ER contact at late endosomes via its FFAT motif. Manipulated by *Salmonella* SifA to decouple LAMP1 acquisition from full maturation, creating the characteristic non-degradative LAMP1⁺ SCV. |
| **PI3P (Phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate)** | A phosphoinositide lipid enriched on early endosomes and phagosomes. Recruits EEA1, Hrs, and other FYVE-domain or PX-domain proteins essential for maturation. Produced by Vps34 kinase (recruited by Rab5). Depleted by *M. tuberculosis* SapM phosphatase to arrest maturation. |
| **PI(4,5)P₂ (Phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate)** | A plasma-membrane-enriched phosphoinositide involved in actin polymerization and signaling. Hydrolyzed by *Salmonella* SopB during phagocytosis to generate PI3P and trigger membrane ruffling. |
| **Vps34** | A class III PI3-kinase that produces PI3P on early endosomes and phagosomes. Recruited by Rab5 to the phagosomal membrane. Part of a complex with Vps15 (regulatory kinase) and Beclin-1 (autophagy link). Inhibited by *M. tuberculosis* LAM which blocks Ca²⁺-dependent activation. |
| **Coat proteins (COPI, COPII, Clathrin)** | Protein complexes that mold membranes into transport vesicles. COPII (Sec23/24 + Sec13/31) drives ER-to-Golgi anterograde transport; COPI (coatomer) drives Golgi-to-ER retrograde retrieval; clathrin (triskelions) mediates endocytosis and TGN-to-endosome traffic. Each coat has a distinct lattice geometry. *Legionella* DrrA recruits COPII vesicles to the LCV. |
| **Arp2/3 complex** | Actin-related protein 2/3 complex — nucleates branched actin filaments at 70° angles, creating the dendritic networks that push membranes forward. Recruited by N-WASP (downstream of Cdc42) or directly by pathogen mimics: *Listeria* ActA and *Shigella* IcsA both activate Arp2/3. The resulting "comet tails" propel bacteria through the cytosol at 10–20 µm/min. |
| **Dynein-dynactin** | A microtubule-associated motor complex that drives retrograde (minus-end-directed) transport toward the microtubule-organizing center (MTOC). The dynactin complex (p150Glued) enhances dynein processivity. RILP-dependent recruitment brings late endosomes and phagosomes to the perinuclear region for lysosomal fusion. |
| **Kinesin-1** | A plus-end-directed microtubule motor. Recruited by *Salmonella* PipB2 to extend Salmonella-induced filaments (SIFs). |

## Tier 4 — Pathogen Subversion Strategies

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Type III Secretion System (T3SS)** | A needle-like molecular syringe (~3.5 MDa) used by Gram-negative bacteria to inject effector proteins directly into the host cell cytosol. Composed of a basal body spanning both membranes, a needle, and a translocon pore in the host membrane. Essential for *Salmonella* (SPI-1 for entry, SPI-2 for intracellular survival), *Shigella* (IpaB/IpaC translocon), and others. Over 200 effectors across species. |
| **Type IV Secretion System (T4SS)** | A secretion system evolutionarily related to bacterial conjugation machinery. The Dot/Icm T4SS of *Legionella pneumophila* is the most effector-rich known system, translocating ~330 effectors into host cells. Also used by *Legionella*, *Coxiella*, and *Brucella*. Requires ATP hydrolysis for substrate translocation. |
| **Type VI Secretion System (T6SS)** | A contractile injection device related to bacteriophage tails. Used by some Gram-negative bacteria to inject effectors into host cells or rival bacteria. *Francisella tularensis* uses IglC/IglD (T6SS components) for phagosomal escape, though its exact mechanism remains debated. |
| **Salmonella Pathogenicity Island 1 (SPI-1)** | A ~40 kb genomic island encoding T3SS-1 and effectors (SopE, SopB, SipA, SipC) that mediate bacterial entry into non-phagocytic cells via the trigger mechanism. Induced by stationary-phase signals (low oxygen, high osmolarity). SPI-1 T3SS is needle-shaped and essential for oral infection of the intestine. |
| **Salmonella Pathogenicity Island 2 (SPI-2)** | A ~40 kb genomic island encoding T3SS-2 and effectors (SifA, SopD2, SseJ, SseF/G, SspH1) required for intracellular survival and SCV maintenance. Induced inside the phagosome (acidic pH, low Mg²⁺). SPI-2 effectors remodulate the SCV to create a replicative niche. |
| **Salmonella-Containing Vacuole (SCV)** | The membrane-bound compartment where *Salmonella* resides and replicates. Acquires LAMP1 (via SifA-SKIP-Rab9 pathway) and Rab7 but excludes M6PR and cathepsins — a unique non-degradative LAMP1⁺ niche. SCV matures into a complex network with SIFs extending into the cytoplasm. pH maintained at ~5.0–5.5 (acidic but non-degradative). |
| **Salmonella-Induced Filaments (SIFs)** | Long tubular membrane extensions (up to 40 µm) emanating from the SCV along microtubules. Enriched in LAMP1, Rab7, and the exocyst complex. Function in nutrient acquisition (amino acids, lipids) and SCV membrane expansion. Require SifA for formation; absent in ΔsifA mutants which have fragmented SCVs. |
| **SifA** | A SPI-2 T3SS effector essential for SCV integrity and SIF formation. Contains a WxxxE motif. Binds SKIP (PLEKHM2), which displaces Rab9 to block M6PR recycling (preventing hydrolase delivery) and recruits kinesin-1 for SIF extension. SifA is also auto-cleaved by its own cysteine protease activity. |
| **Listeriolysin O (LLO)** | A cholesterol-dependent cytolysin (CDC) secreted by *Listeria monocytogenes*. Pore-forming toxin that creates ~30 nm pores in host membranes. Has an acidic pH optimum (~5.5) — active in the early phagosome but auto-inactivated at neutral cytosolic pH (~7.2), providing a built-in safety switch after escape. Works with PlcA and PlcB (phospholipases) for complete vacuolar lysis. |
| **ActA** | A *Listeria* surface protein (1023 aa) that mimics host WASP-family proteins via its N-terminal domain. Polarised to one bacterial pole by SecA2-dependent secretion. Nucleates Arp2/3-dependent branched actin polymerization, creating "comet tails" that propel the bacterium through the cytosol at 10–20 µm/min. Also mediates cell-to-cell spread via double-membrane protrusions that are phagocytosed by neighbouring cells. |
| **InlA / InlB** | *Listeria* invasins that trigger zipper-mediated entry. InlA (leucine-rich repeat) binds E-cadherin (epithelial cell junction protein); InlB (GW repeats) binds the Met receptor (HGF receptor). Both trigger PI3K activation and Rac1-dependent actin polymerization for membrane cup formation around the bacterium. InlB also binds gC1qR and integrins. |
| **Lipoarabinomannan (LAM)** | A major glycolipid of the *M. tuberculosis* cell wall (~2 mg per 10⁹ bacteria). Inhibits Ca²⁺ signaling and blocks Vps34 recruitment to the phagosome, preventing PI3P production. Mannose-capped LAM (ManLAM) also inhibits phagosome-lysosome fusion by blocking calcium/calmodulin-dependent phagosome maturation. |
| **SapM** | A secreted acid phosphatase of *M. tuberculosis* active at pH 5.0–6.0. Dephosphorylates PI3P on the phagosomal membrane, preventing Rab5→Rab7 conversion and arresting maturation. Requires ESX-1-mediated phagosomal membrane permeabilization to access its lipid substrate on the luminal face. |
| **MptpA / MptpB** | *M. tuberculosis* secreted protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs). MptpA dephosphorylates VPS33B (a HOPS subunit) to block V-ATPase trafficking, maintaining near-neutral phagosomal pH (~6.4). MptpB hydrolyzes PI(3,5)P₂ and binds host VPS9-ankyrin repeat protein. Both are non-essential in culture but required for virulence in vivo. |
| **ESX-1 (Type VII Secretion System)** | A secretion system in *M. tuberculosis* that permeabilizes the phagosomal membrane via the ESXA/ESPB translocon. Enables effectors like SapM to access host cytosolic targets. Required for virulence — Δesx-1 mutants (like BCG) are avirulent. Also triggers innate immune detection via the cGAS-STING pathway when cytosolic DNA leaks through the pore. |
| **Legionella-Containing Vacuole (LCV)** | The replicative niche of *Legionella pneumophila*. An ER-derived compartment that bypasses the endocytic pathway entirely, built by ~330 Dot/Icm T4SS effectors within minutes of entry. Recruits ER markers (calnexin, Sec22b), Mitochondria, Golgi vesicles, and ribosomes. Avoids endosomal fusion by excluding Rab5 and Rab7 from the LCV membrane. |
| **DrrA / SidM** | A *Legionella* Dot/Icm effector with remarkable multifunctionality. Acts as a GEF (activates Rab1 by exchanging GDP→GTP), a GDF (displaces GDI to extract Rab1 from cytosol), and an AMPylase (adds AMP to Rab1, locking it in GTP-bound state). Collectively, these activities redirect ER-derived COPII vesicles to the LCV. Countered by SidD (deAMPylase) and Lem3 (dephosphocholinase). |
| **AnkX** | A *Legionella* effector with a FIC (filamentation induced by cAMP) domain that phosphocholinates Rab1 and Rab35 using CDP-choline as the donor substrate. This post-translational modification prevents GEF activation and GDI-mediated extraction, locking Rabs in an inactive membrane-associated state. Reversed by Lem3 (dephosphocholinase) during infection for temporal control. |
| **IcsA (VirG)** | A *Shigella* outer membrane autotransporter (1102 aa) that recruits N-WASP and Arp2/3 to the bacterial pole via its type I secretion signal. Creates a unipolar actin comet tail for intracellular motility (~20 µm/min). Also mediates invasion of epithelial cells by engaging α-catenin at cell-cell junctions. |
| **Septin cage** | A host cytoskeletal structure composed of septin filaments (SEPT2, SEPT6, SEPT7) that entraps cytosolic *Shigella* at ~1–2 µm diameter. Restricts actin-based motility by caging the bacterium and targets it for autophagy by exposing ubiquitinated surfaces. Counteracted by IcsB which masks IcsA from LC3 recognition. |
| **Xenophagy** | A form of selective autophagy that targets intracellular pathogens for lysosomal degradation. ubiquitin coats the pathogen; adaptor proteins (p62/SQSTM1, NDP52, OPTN) bridge ubiquitin to LC3 on autophagosomal membranes. Several pathogens evade xenophagy: *Listeria* (ActA recruits Arp2/3 instead of LC3), *Shigella* (IcsB masks IcsA), *Salmonella* (SseL deubiquitinates cytosolic bacteria). |

## Tier 5 — Experimental Techniques & Analysis

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Fluorescence microscopy** | A technique using fluorophores (GFP, mCherry, antibodies) to visualize specific proteins or structures in fixed or live cells. Excitation light at one wavelength excites the fluorophore; emitted light at a longer wavelength is detected. Used to track phagosome markers (Rab5-GFP, LAMP1-mCherry) and pathogen localization in real time. Resolution limited to ~200 nm (diffraction limit). |
| **Confocal microscopy** | A fluorescence microscopy method using a pinhole aperture to reject out-of-focus light, generating thin optical sections (~0.5 µm). Essential for resolving membrane boundaries and 3D reconstruction of phagosome-organelle contacts. Laser-scanning confocal acquires one pixel at a time; spinning-disk confocal is faster (parallelized) for live imaging. |
| **Widefield fluorescence** | A microscopy method where the entire sample is illuminated uniformly; faster and higher throughput than confocal but includes out-of-focus blur. Suitable for endpoint colocalization assays and high-content screening. Deconvolution algorithms can partially remove out-of-focus light computationally. |
| **Colocalization analysis** | A quantitative method to measure the spatial overlap between two fluorescence channels. Indicates whether two proteins (e.g., a pathogen and LAMP1) occupy the same subcellular compartment. Requires proper controls (single-channel images to assess bleed-through) and statistical analysis (see Pearson's, Manders'). |
| **Pearson's correlation coefficient** | A colocalization metric ranging from -1 to +1 that measures the linear correlation between pixel intensities in two channels. Values >0.5 indicate significant colocalization; ~1.0 means perfect overlap. Sensitive to background noise and requires proper thresholding. |
| **Manders' colocalization coefficients** | Coefficients (M1, M2) that quantify the fraction of one channel's signal that overlaps with the other. M1 = fraction of channel 1 overlapping channel 2; M2 = vice versa. Less sensitive to intensity variations than Pearson's. Useful when one protein is much brighter than the other. |
| **Galectin-3 (GAL3) puncta** | A host lectin that binds exposed β-galactoside residues on the luminal face of damaged vacuolar membranes. Under normal conditions, these glycans are hidden inside intact organelles. When a pathogen lyses the phagosome (e.g., *Listeria* LLO, *Shigella* IpaB), GAL3 rapidly binds the damage site, forming visible puncta. Used as the gold-standard assay for phagosomal escape. |
| **Live-cell imaging** | Time-lapse microscopy of living cells, tracking dynamic processes like Rab conversion, phagosome movement, and pathogen escape in real time. Requires temperature-controlled stages (37°C), CO₂ control, and photo-stable fluorophores (mNeonGreen, mScarlet). Frame rates of 1–30 seconds capture Rab5→Rab7 conversion dynamics. |
| **Fluorescence tagging** | The fusion of a fluorescent protein (GFP, mCherry, mNeonGreen) to a protein of interest (e.g., GFP-Rab5, mCherry-LAMP1) to visualize its localization and dynamics. Must preserve protein function — check that tagged constructs rescue knockout phenotypes. Alternative: HaloTag or SNAP-tag for covalent labelling with synthetic fluorophores. |
| **Immunofluorescence (IF)** | Detection of proteins in fixed cells using primary antibodies and fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies. Allows multiplexed labeling (4–6 channels) of host and pathogen proteins. Fixation method matters: methanol preserves epitopes but extracts lipids; paraformaldehyde crosslinks but may mask epitopes. Antigen retrieval may be needed. |
| **RNA interference (RNAi)** | A method to knock down gene expression using small interfering RNAs (siRNAs). The siRNA (21–23 nt) is loaded into RISC, which cleaves complementary mRNA. Used to test whether a host factor (e.g., SKIP, Rab7, VPS33B) is required for pathogen entry, trafficking, or replication. Knockdown efficiency should be verified by qRT-PCR or Western blot (typically 70–90% reduction). |
| **Z-stack** | A series of optical sections acquired at different focal planes (typically 0.2–0.5 µm apart). Reconstructed into a 3D volume to analyze phagosome position, shape, and contact sites. Essential for quantifying the spatial relationship between pathogen vacuoles and ER, mitochondria, or recycling endosomes. |
| **Electron microscopy (EM)** | Uses electron beams instead of light to achieve nanometer resolution (~0.1 nm for TEM, ~1 nm for SEM). Transmission EM (TEM) reveals internal ultrastructure of phagosomes — SIFs, LCV ribosome coating, phagosomal membrane integrity. Scanning EM (SEM) shows surface topology — membrane ruffles, bacterial surface structures. Requires fixation (glutaraldehyde + osmium tetroxide), dehydration, resin embedding, and ultrathin sectioning (~70 nm). |
| **Proximity ligation assay (PLA)** | Detects protein-protein interactions in situ with ~40 nm resolution. Uses paired primary antibodies + secondary antibodies conjugated to complementary DNA oligonucleotides. When in proximity (<40 nm), oligonucleotides ligate and are rolling-circle amplified, producing a fluorescent dot. Used to confirm effector-host protein contacts (e.g., SifA-SKIP on the SCV). |
| **CRISPR-Cas9 knockout** | Genome editing to create null mutations in host genes. Guide RNA directs Cas9 nuclease to cut at a specific genomic site; non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) introduces insertions/deletions that disrupt the reading frame. Used to test whether a host factor (e.g., Rab7, SKIP, VPS33B) is required for pathogen entry, vacuolar trafficking, or replication. |
| **Flow cytometry (FACS)** | High-throughput single-cell analysis and sorting using fluorescent labels. Cells pass single-file through a laser beam; forward scatter (size), side scatter (complexity), and fluorescence are measured. Quantifies phagocytic uptake, pathogen burden, and host marker expression across 10,000+ cells/minute. FACS sorting can isolate infected vs. uninfected populations for downstream RNA-seq or proteomics. |
| **Western blot** | Protein detection by SDS-PAGE separation (molecular weight), membrane transfer (PVDF or nitrocellulose), and antibody probing. Used to confirm effector expression, post-translational modifications (e.g., Rab AMPylation by DrrA), and protein degradation during infection. Semi-quantitative; loading controls (β-actin, GAPDH) are essential. |
| **Quantitative PCR (qPCR)** | Real-time PCR that amplifies and simultaneously detects DNA using fluorescent probes (TaqMan) or intercalating dyes (SYBR Green). Used to measure pathogen burden (16S rRNA gene copies), gene expression changes (fold-change by ΔΔCt method), and RNAi knockdown efficiency. |
| **Mass spectrometry (MS)** | Analytical technique that ionises molecules and separates them by mass-to-charge ratio. Used for proteomics (identifying interacting proteins via immunoprecipitation-MS), metabolomics, and post-translational modification mapping (e.g., Rab AMPylation sites). |
| **Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)** | A technique to isolate a protein and its binding partners using antibody-conjugated beads. The target protein (bait) is captured from cell lysates; associated proteins (prey) co-precipitate. Confirmed by Western blot or MS. Used to validate effector-host protein interactions identified by yeast two-hybrid or affinity purification. |
| **Yeast two-hybrid (Y2H)** | A genetic screen for protein-protein interactions in *S. cerevisiae*. Bait and prey are fused to DNA-binding and activation domains of a transcription factor, respectively. Interaction reconstitutes the transcription factor, activating a reporter gene (HIS3, LacZ). Used to map effector-host protein interaction networks. |

## Tier 6 — Computational & Bioinformatics Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **SQLite** | A lightweight, serverless, self-contained relational database engine. Stores data in a single `.db` file. Used here as the primary data store (`hostpathogen.db`) because it requires no running server, works offline, and ships inside the Vercel serverless bundle. All queries use parameterised SQL with `?` placeholders to prevent injection. |
| **pandas DataFrame** | A two-dimensional, labelled data structure in Python (like a spreadsheet in code). The `to_df()` helper in `loader.py` returns query results as DataFrames, enabling column selection, groupby, merge, and vectorised operations. Central to feature extraction in the ML pipeline. |
| **NumPy** | The fundamental numerical computing library for Python. Provides multidimensional arrays and vectorised math. Used throughout the ML modules for feature matrices, random number generation, log-transforms, and array manipulation. |
| **NetworkX** | A Python library for graph/network analysis. Stores nodes (with attributes) and edges (with weights/types). Used to build the bipartite host-pathogen interactome graph, compute centrality metrics (degree, betweenness), identify hub proteins, and extract per-pathogen subgraphs. |
| **scikit-learn** | The primary machine learning library in Python. Provides classifiers (RandomForest, SVM, KNN, LogisticRegression), dimensionality reduction (PCA), preprocessing (StandardScaler), model evaluation (StratifiedKFold, confusion_matrix, classification_report), and hyperparameter tuning (GridSearchCV). |
| **RandomForest (RF)** | An ensemble classifier that builds many decision trees on random subsets of features and data, then votes on the prediction. Used here to predict pathogen evasion strategy from 11 effector-derived features. Robust to overfitting on small datasets (54 samples). |
| **Support Vector Machine (SVM)** | A classifier that finds the optimal hyperplane separating classes in high-dimensional space. Compared against RF, KNN, and Logistic Regression in the classifier comparison endpoint. |
| **K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)** | A non-parametric classifier that assigns the majority class among the K closest training samples. Simple baseline for the classifier comparison. |
| **Logistic Regression** | A linear model for binary/multiclass classification using the softmax function. Used as a baseline classifier in the comparison pipeline. |
| **PCA (Principal Component Analysis)** | A linear dimensionality reduction technique that projects data onto axes of maximum variance (principal components). Used to visualise how infected vs. control host cells cluster in gene expression space, and how pathogens cluster by effector feature profiles. |
| **UMAP (Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection)** | A nonlinear dimensionality reduction technique that preserves both local neighbourhood structure and global data geometry. Produces more interpretable clusters than PCA for complex biological data. Requires the `umap-learn` package. |
| **StandardScaler** | A preprocessing step that centres each feature to zero mean and unit variance. Required before PCA because PCA is sensitive to feature scale — features with larger numeric ranges would dominate otherwise. |
| **Hypergeometric distribution** | A discrete probability distribution describing the number of successes in draws without replacement from a finite population. Used for over-representation analysis (ORA): given N total proteins, K in a pathway, n proteins in your gene list, what is the probability of seeing k or more overlap? Equivalent to Fisher's exact test. |
| **Fisher's exact test** | An exact statistical test for enrichment in a 2×2 contingency table. Used interchangeably with the hypergeometric test for pathway enrichment. Computationally expensive for large tables but exact for small sample sizes. |
| **Over-representation analysis (ORA)** | A pathway analysis method that tests whether a list of genes/proteins is enriched for a particular pathway compared to a random sample from the background. Uses the hypergeometric test with multiple testing correction. |
| **Bonferroni correction** | The simplest multiple testing correction: multiply each p-value by the number of tests performed. Controls the family-wise error rate but is very conservative (high false negative rate). Used in `enrichment.py`. |
| **False Discovery Rate (FDR / BH)** | A less conservative multiple testing correction (Benjamini-Hochberg) that controls the expected proportion of false positives among significant results. More commonly used than Bonferroni in genomics. |
| **DESeq2** | An R/Bioconductor package for differential expression analysis of RNA-seq count data. Uses a negative binomial model to identify genes that are significantly up- or down-regulated between conditions (e.g. infected vs. control). Produces log2 fold-changes and adjusted p-values. |
| **Log2 fold-change (log2FC)** | The base-2 logarithm of the ratio of expression between two conditions. A log2FC of +2 means 4× upregulation; -2 means 4× downregulation. Symmetric around zero and additive, making it suitable for statistical testing and visualisation (volcano plots). |
| **Adjusted p-value** | A p-value corrected for multiple hypothesis testing. In DESeq2, uses the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure. A gene with adjusted p-value < 0.05 is considered significantly differentially expressed after accounting for the thousands of genes tested simultaneously. |
| **Volcano plot** | A scatter plot of log2 fold-change (x-axis) vs. -log10(adjusted p-value) (y-axis). Genes in the upper-left and upper-right corners are significantly down- and up-regulated, respectively. A standard visualisation for differential expression results. |
| **MA plot** | A scatter plot of mean expression strength (x-axis, the "A" = average axis) vs. log2 fold-change (y-axis, the "M" = minus axis). Reveals where expression changes occur across the abundance range and flags bias from low-abundance genes. Used in the My Data Differential Expression tab. |
| **Welch's t-test** | A two-sample t-test that does **not** assume equal variances between the two groups. Common when group sizes or variances differ. Used in the My Data Compare and Differential Expression tabs (e.g. infected vs. control). |
| **Box plot** | A five-number summary of a distribution: median line, interquartile-range (Q1–Q3) box, and whiskers to the min/max. The My Data Compare tab draws one box per group for a chosen response column. |
| **z-score (standard score)** | A value standardised by its own distribution: z = (x − mean) / SD. In the My Data Heatmap and Differential Expression tabs, values are z-scored per row so each feature's colour reflects its relative up/down pattern across samples regardless of absolute scale. |
| **Dot (bubble) plot** | A scatter plot where point size and/or colour encode additional variables. The My Data Enrichment tab uses one with gene ratio on the x-axis, pathway on the y-axis, bubble size = number of hits, and colour = −log10(adjusted p-value). |
| **Heatmap** | A matrix visualisation where values are encoded as colours. Used for gene expression data where rows are genes, columns are samples, and colour intensity represents expression level. Hierarchical clustering on both axes reveals co-regulated gene modules. |
| **Multiple sequence alignment (MSA)** | The arrangement of three or more biological sequences (DNA/protein) to identify regions of similarity that may indicate functional, structural, or evolutionary relationships. Biopython's `MultipleSeqAlignment` is used here for effector protein sequences. |
| **Neighbour-joining (NJ)** | A distance-based method for constructing phylogenetic trees. Computes a pairwise distance matrix between sequences, then iteratively joins the closest pairs while adjusting for evolutionary rate differences. Faster than maximum likelihood but less statistically rigorous. |
| **Newick format** | A standard text format for representing phylogenetic trees using nested parentheses. Example: `((A:0.1,B:0.2):0.3,C:0.4);`. Parsed by the frontend `phylogeny.js` module to render SVG trees. |
| **Biopython** | A Python library for computational biology. Provides modules for sequence I/O (`SeqIO`), alignment (`Align`), phylogenetics (`Phylo`), and BLAST. Used in `phylogenetics.py` for MSA and tree construction. |
| **BioSQL** | A database schema for storing biological sequences in a relational database. Not used directly here, but the project's SQLite approach mirrors BioSQL's philosophy of structured biological data storage. |
| **FASTA** | A text-based format for representing nucleotide or protein sequences. Each sequence has a header line starting with `>` followed by the raw sequence data. The effector sequences for phylogenetics are stored in `r/data/effector_sequences.fasta`. |
| **FASTA header** | The line in a FASTA file starting with `>` that contains the sequence identifier and optional description. Conventionally: `>sp|Q9CAW9|SIFA_SALTM SifA OS=Salmonella enterica`. |
| **FastAPI** | A modern, high-performance Python web framework for building REST APIs. Uses Python type hints for automatic validation, serialization, and OpenAPI documentation generation. The entire API is a single file (`api/index.py`) with ~20 endpoints. |
| **Pydantic** | A data validation library for Python that uses type hints. FastAPI uses Pydantic models (`BaseModel`) to validate request bodies and serialize responses. Example: `MarkerInput` validates that POST requests contain a `markers: List[str]` field. |
| **Uvicorn** | A lightning-fast ASGI server for Python. Runs the FastAPI app locally with hot-reload (`--reload`). In production, Vercel handles this via its serverless runtime. |
| **REST API** | Representational State Transfer — an architectural style for web services using HTTP methods (GET, POST, PUT, DELETE) on resource-oriented URLs. The project's API follows REST conventions: `GET /api/pathogens` lists, `GET /api/pathogens/{name}` retrieves. |
| **JSON (JavaScript Object Notation)** | A lightweight, text-based data interchange format. Used for API responses, the `fallback.json` offline dataset, chart data in `data/charts/`, and the embedded `TOOLKIT_DATA` in `js/data.js`. |
| **CDN (Content Delivery Network)** | A geographically distributed network of servers that caches and serves static content close to users. Vercel's CDN serves `index.html`, `css/`, `js/`, and `img/` globally. The API runs on Vercel's serverless edge, not the CDN. |
| **Serverless** | A cloud execution model where the provider dynamically manages server allocation. The FastAPI app runs as a Vercel serverless function — it spins up on demand, handles a request, and shuts down. Cold starts may add latency; the module-level caches mitigate this. |
| **Thread-local storage** | A programming pattern where each thread has its own independent copy of a variable. Used in `loader.py` to give each request thread its own SQLite connection, avoiding thread-safety issues with shared connections. |
| **Connection pooling** | A technique for reusing database connections instead of opening/closing them per query. The `loader.py` module caches one connection per thread in `threading.local()`, verifying it with `SELECT 1` before reuse. |
| **Parameterised query** | A SQL query where user-provided values are bound separately (via `?` placeholders) rather than interpolated into the query string. Prevents SQL injection attacks. All queries in this project use parameterised forms. |
| **EDA (Exploratory Data Analysis)** | The initial phase of data analysis involving summary statistics, visualisations, and pattern discovery. The Jupyter notebooks (01–04) are structured as EDA workflows for the host-pathogen dataset. |
| **Feature engineering** | The process of creating input variables (features) from raw data for machine learning. Here, effector metadata is transformed into 11 numeric features (counts of T3SS, T4SS, toxins, etc.) per pathogen for the classifier. |
| **Feature matrix** | A 2D array where rows are samples (pathogens) and columns are features (numeric attributes). The classifier uses a 54×11 feature matrix. Each row summarises a pathogen's effector repertoire. |
| **Confusion matrix** | A table showing true vs. predicted class labels. Rows are actual classes, columns are predicted. Diagonal entries are correct predictions; off-diagonal are errors. Used to evaluate the RandomForest classifier. |
| **Classification report** | A summary table of precision, recall, F1-score, and support per class. Precision = TP/(TP+FP), Recall = TP/(TP+FN), F1 = harmonic mean. Generated by scikit-learn's `classification_report()`. |
| **StratifiedKFold** | A cross-validation strategy that preserves the percentage of samples for each class in each fold. Essential for imbalanced datasets where some classes have fewer samples (e.g., "extracellular" has only a few pathogens). |
| **Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV)** | A cross-validation strategy where each sample is used once as the test set while the rest form the training set. For 54 pathogens, this means 54 train/test splits. More comprehensive than k-fold for small datasets. |
| **Grid search** | An exhaustive search over specified hyperparameter values. For the RandomForest, grid search tests combinations of `n_estimators` (100–500), `max_depth` (None, 10, 20, 30), and `min_samples_split` (2, 5, 10) to find the best configuration. |
| **One-hot encoding** | A categorical-to-numeric transformation where each category becomes a binary (0/1) column. Used when converting categorical features to numeric for ML, though the current classifier uses count-based features instead. |
| **Label encoding** | Converting categorical labels to integers. The evasion strategies ("arrest", "escape", etc.) are mapped to numeric class labels for classifier training. |
| **Biopython Phylo** | The phylogenetics module of Biopython. `Phylo.read()` parses Newick/NHX trees; `Phylo.draw()` renders them as ASCII or matplotlib plots. The `DistanceTreeConstructor` builds NJ trees from distance matrices. |
| **Distance matrix** | A symmetric matrix of pairwise evolutionary distances between sequences. In NJ tree construction, the distance calculator computes this from the MSA, and the tree constructor uses it to build the phylogeny. |

## Tier 7 — Project-Specific Technical Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|

Contains all 6 tables (pathogens, effectors, host_proteins, effector_targets, maturation_stages, stage_markers). Read by the Python package via `loader.py`, exported to JSON by `export_fallback_json.py`, and shipped inside the Vercel serverless bundle via `vercel.json` `includeFiles`. |

Enables the frontend to work fully offline or from `file://` without any server. The `data-loader.js` module checks for this as a fallback when `fetch()` fails. |
| **data-loader.js** | The frontend data access layer. Tries `fetch()` from the API endpoints first; on failure (or `file://` protocol), falls back to `TOOLKIT_DATA`. All chart/graph modules import data through this layer. |
| **build_db.py** | The database build script at `src/hostpathogen/data/build_db.py`. Reads the 6 seed CSVs, creates the schema (DDL), and inserts all data. Run as `python src/hostpathogen/data/build_db.py`. Produces `hostpathogen.db`. |
| **export_fallback_json.py** | The frontend data export script at `scripts/export_fallback_json.py`. Reads from `hostpathogen.db`, runs PCA/UMAP/phylogeny/classifier computations, and writes both `data/fallback.json` and `js/data.js` (with `TOOLKIT_DATA`). |
| **export_chart_data.R** | The R data export script at `r/export_chart_data.R`. Reads from `hostpathogen.db` and writes CSVs, FASTA files, and Newick trees to `r/data/` for R analysis scripts. |
| **export_r.py** | The Python-to-R data export at `src/hostpathogen/data/export_r.py`. Exports DB tables as CSVs in a format R can read directly. |
| **refresh_data.py** | The end-to-end data refresh script at `scripts/refresh_data.py`. Optionally pulls latest data from PHI-base (EBI API), UniProt REST API, and IntAct molecular interaction database. Updates seed CSVs and rebuilds the DB. |
| **vercel.json** | Vercel deployment configuration. Defines URL rewrites (`/api/*` → `api/index.py`, everything else → `index.html`) and function settings (60s timeout, includes `src/**` in the bundle). |
| **pyproject.toml** | Python project metadata. Declares the `hostpathogen` package (v0.3.0), requires Python ≥3.11, and specifies dev extras (`pytest`, `jupyter`). The `[tool.setuptools.packages.find]` section points to `src/`. |
| **PhagosomeMaturation class** | The core trafficking module at `src/hostpathogen/trafficking.py`. Models 5 maturation stages as a state machine. Methods: `get_stage(markers)` → stage name, `stage_info(name)` → dict with pH/time/markers/pathogens, `trajectory(snapshots)` → list of stage transitions. |
| **Bipartite graph** | A graph with two disjoint node sets where edges only connect nodes from different sets. The interactome uses effector nodes (prefixed `E:`) and host protein nodes (prefixed `H:`), with edges representing targeting relationships. Built by `interactome.py` using NetworkX. |
| **Hub protein** | A host protein targeted by many different pathogens' effectors. Identified by degree centrality in the bipartite interactome graph. High-degree hubs (e.g., Rab7, Actin, Rac1) represent common viral/bacterial exploitation points — potential drug targets. |
| **Feature matrix** | The 54×11 numeric array built by `ml/classifier.py:extract_features()`. Rows = pathogens, columns = count-based features (n_effectors, n_targets, n_T3SS, n_T4SS, n_T6SS, n_toxins, n_surface_proteins, n_invasins, n_pathways, n_localizations, n_interaction_types). |
| **Interaction type** | The mechanism by which an effector engages its host target. Stored in `effector_targets.csv` as free text: "GEF" (guanine nucleotide exchange factor), "GAP" (GTPase-activating protein), "binds", "inhibits", "dephosphorylates", "recruits", "pore-forming", etc. |
| **Marker snapshot** | A list of host protein marker names representing the molecular state of a phagosome at a single time point. Sent as JSON to `POST /api/trafficking/predict-stage`. Example: `{"markers": ["Rab5", "EEA1", "PI3P"]}` → "Early phagosome". |
| **Stage markers** | The 7 host proteins used to define maturation stages: Rab5, Rab7, EEA1, PI3P, LAMP1, V-ATPase, RILP. Each stage has a binary presence/absence profile (1 = present, 0 = absent). Stored in `stage_markers.csv`. |
| **Seed CSVs** | The source-of-truth data files in `src/hostpathogen/data/seed/`. Six CSV files defining pathogens, effectors, host_proteins, effector_targets, maturation_stages, and stage_markers. Edits here propagate to the DB, API, and frontend via the build pipeline. |
| **thread-local connection** | The SQLite connection management pattern in `loader.py`. Each Python thread gets its own `sqlite3.Connection` stored in `threading.local()`. Connections are reused within a thread and verified with `SELECT 1` before use. Prevents cross-thread sharing of non-thread-safe SQLite connections. |
| **Module-level cache** | A pattern where expensive computations (e.g., `build_network()`, `train_classifier()`, `_build_marker_profiles()`) are computed once and stored in a module-level global variable (e.g., `_cached_network`). Subsequent calls return the cached result. Critical for Vercel cold-start performance. |
| **Cold start** | The delay when a serverless function is invoked after being idle. Vercel spins up a new container, loads the Python runtime, imports modules, and runs module-level code. The project's module-level caches (NetworkX graph, trained model) add to cold-start time but prevent repeated computation. |
| **Include files** | The `vercel.json` `includeFiles: "src/**"` setting tells Vercel to bundle the entire `src/` directory (including `hostpathogen.db`) into the serverless function package. Without this, the DB would not be available at runtime. |
| **Chart data JSON** | Pre-computed datasets in `data/charts/` (e.g., strategy counts, effector distributions, stage timelines). Generated by `export_fallback_json.py` and consumed by `charts.js` for static visualisations that don't require API calls. |
| **Newick tree string** | The phylogenetic tree representation returned by `GET /api/ml/phylogeny` and embedded in `TOOLKIT_DATA`. Parsed by `js/phylogeny.js` which converts the nested-parenthesis format into an SVG with labelled branches and coloured clades. |
| **PCA coordinates** | The 2D coordinates returned by `GET /api/ml/pca` and `GET /api/ml/pathogen-pca`. Each point represents a sample (host cell or pathogen) projected onto the first two principal components. Used by `ml-plots.js` to render scatter plots. |
| **UMAP parameters** | The `n_neighbors` and `min_dist` parameters that control UMAP behaviour. `n_neighbors` (default 5) controls local vs. global structure preservation; `min_dist` (default 0.3) controls how tightly points cluster. Exposed as query parameters on `GET /api/ml/umap`. |
| **LOOCV report** | The detailed leave-one-out cross-validation output from `GET /api/ml/cross-validate`. Contains per-fold predictions, overall accuracy, and per-class precision/recall. More informative than a single accuracy number. |
| **Grid search results** | The hyperparameter tuning output from `GET /api/ml/grid-search`. Tests combinations of RF parameters and returns the best configuration with cross-validated accuracy scores. |
| **Database schema** | The formal definition of tables, columns, types, and constraints in `hostpathogen.db`. Defined in `build_db.py` as `SCHEMA_SQL`. Uses `INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT` for IDs, `TEXT` for strings, `REAL` for floats, and `FOREIGN KEY` constraints with indexes. |
| **idx_effectors_pathogen** | A database index on `effectors(pathogen_id)` that speeds up queries joining effectors to pathogens. Created in `build_db.py` alongside the schema. Without this index, per-pathogen effector queries would scan the full ~250-row table. |
| **idx_et_host_protein** | A database index on `effector_targets(host_protein_id)` that speeds up reverse lookups: "which effectors target this host protein?" Used by the hub analysis and per-protein detail endpoints. |
